# Research Unit

ID: R008
Title: Cantor — uniqueness of trigonometric series, exceptional sets, and point-set questions
Period: approximately 1860–1885

## Purpose

Open the route from the existing Fourier/Dirichlet/Riemann analysis line toward set-theoretic questions through a concrete mathematical problem: uniqueness of trigonometric representation and the role of exceptional point sets.

## Central Atlas Question

**When is a trigonometric representation unique, and what kinds of exceptional sets can be tolerated?**

## Historical scope

Investigate:

1. The uniqueness problem for trigonometric series as Cantor encountered it.
2. The roles of Riemann, Heine, and other intermediaries in the problem's development.
3. What Cantor's early results actually say about sets of exceptional points.
4. When derived sets and related point-set ideas enter, and in what terminology.
5. How far one can historically connect these investigations to Cantor's later set theory.
6. Which familiar narrative — "Fourier series led to set theory" — is supported, overstated, or retrospective.

## Origin pattern

Primarily **internal mathematical development**:

representation/convergence/uniqueness problem → exceptional sets → new questions about sets of points.

This unit should be a test case for the guideline that mathematics can generate new Questions internally without an external application at the front of the Story.

## Integration target

Strong expected intersections with:

- R001 Fourier representation;
- R003 Dirichlet convergence/admissibility;
- R004 Riemann trigonometric/integration work.

The unit should create a reviewed handoff from representation/convergence to uniqueness/exceptional-set questions, not a generic "Cantor follows Riemann" edge.

## Historical risks to test

- "Cantor invented set theory while studying Fourier series" as an unqualified causal claim.
- Conflating uniqueness sets, exceptional sets, derived sets, and later abstract set theory.
- Projecting modern point-set topology terminology backward.
- Omitting Heine or other intermediaries.
- Treating later Cantorian cardinality work as already implicit in the earliest uniqueness papers.

## Expected Story direction

Candidate Story:

**When is a representation unique?**

Possible arc:

trigonometric representation → uniqueness problem → exceptional points → structured sets of points → new internal Questions about sets.

## Boundaries

Do not absorb:

- full transfinite cardinal arithmetic;
- continuum hypothesis;
- general topology;
- measure theory;
- philosophical/foundational debates about actual infinity except where needed to interpret the researched period.
