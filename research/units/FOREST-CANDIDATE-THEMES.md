# Forest-level candidate research themes

Status: planning candidates, not approved Research Units unless separately promoted to an `Rxxx-*.md` brief.

## Selection principle

After R005, do not select units only by following the immediately preceding person or concept. Select for the shape of the whole Atlas:

- open missing major trunks;
- deepen a trunk only when it creates a meaningful new mathematical question;
- create historically evidenced intersections rather than synthetic genealogies;
- include external scientific/engineering contexts when they generated or sharpened mathematical methods/questions, while keeping the Atlas Question mathematical;
- periodically return to early roots so later units do not float without historical ancestry.

See `research/EXTERNAL-CONTEXT-GUIDELINE.md` for outside-mathematics contexts.

## Strong next-trunk candidates

### Geometry opening — Gauss and intrinsic curvature

Possible Atlas Question:

**What information about a surface is intrinsic to the surface itself?**

Research focus:

- curvature of surfaces and the Theorema Egregium;
- what Gauss actually established and in what context;
- distinction between surveying/geodesy context and the mathematical Question;
- later Riemannian interpretation must not be projected backward as Gauss's own framework.

Graph role: open a Geometry trunk that can later meet Riemann, manifolds, topology, and differential geometry.

Suggested position: strong candidate for R007, subject to a separate approved brief.

### Cantor — uniqueness of trigonometric series and point sets

Possible Atlas Question:

**When is a trigonometric representation unique, and how large can an exceptional set be?**

Research focus:

- uniqueness questions for trigonometric series;
- Heine and other intermediaries;
- exceptional points and derived sets;
- the actual historical route from these investigations to Cantor's point-set work;
- explicitly test the slogan that Fourier series "created set theory".

Graph role: branch from the existing Fourier/Riemann analysis line toward point-set and foundational questions.

Suggested position: strong candidate for R008 if R007 opens Geometry.

### Dedekind — factorization and ideals

Possible Atlas Question:

**How can unique factorization be recovered when ordinary factorization fails?**

Research focus:

- algebraic integers and failure of unique factorization;
- ideal numbers / ideals and relevant intermediaries;
- Dedekind/Kummer/Kronecker distinctions;
- avoid projecting modern ring/ideal language backward without qualification.

Graph role: deepen the Algebra trunk after R006 and open algebraic number theory.

### Lebesgue — measure and integration

Possible Atlas Question:

**What should integration mean for functions that escape the useful reach of Riemann's framework?**

Research focus:

- limitations actually perceived in late nineteenth-century integration;
- measure, sets, and integration;
- relation to Riemann and Cantor without assuming a simple linear genealogy.

Graph role: reconnect point-set questions with Analysis.

## Early-root / external-context candidates

These are important because R001 currently begins in 1807 even though several major mathematical-problem lineages start earlier.

### Kepler / Galileo / Newton — from observed motion to mathematical dynamics

External contexts:

- planetary observations and Keplerian regularities;
- terrestrial/projectile motion.

Possible Atlas Questions:

- **How can changing motion be represented mathematically?**
- **How can one mathematical law account for apparently different motions?**

Research focus:

- do not label Kepler as doing differential equations;
- distinguish empirical/mathematical regularities from Newtonian dynamical explanation;
- test rather than assume direct transmission and unification narratives;
- follow only enough astronomy/mechanics to establish the mathematical transition.

Graph role: provide an early root for calculus, mechanics, and later differential-equation/mathematical-physics branches.

### Vibrating strings — Euler, d'Alembert, Bernoulli and admissible functions

External context: vibrating strings / continuous media.

Possible Atlas Questions:

- **What counts as an admissible function in a differential-equation solution?**
- **Can an arbitrary shape be represented by a trigonometric expression?**

Research focus:

- competing meanings of "function" before Fourier;
- wave equation and solution forms only as needed for the mathematical controversy;
- avoid treating later Fourier-series theory as already present.

Graph role: a pre-Fourier intersection among differential equations, representation, and function concepts.

### Potential theory / Green

External contexts: gravitation and electrostatics.

Possible Atlas Questions:

- **How can boundary information determine a field inside a region?**
- **What mathematical object captures the influence of a source or boundary?**

Research focus:

- Green's essay and historical reception;
- boundary-value reasoning and integral identities;
- later PDE terminology must be dated carefully.

Graph role: deepen the mathematical-physics/PDE tributary without turning the Atlas into a physics history.

### Maxwell / Heaviside — operational methods under engineering pressure

External contexts:

- electromagnetism;
- telegraphy and transmission problems.

Possible Atlas Questions:

- **When may a differential operator be manipulated algebraically?**
- **What makes a formally successful operation mathematically legitimate?**

Research focus:

- what Heaviside actually did operationally and why;
- which parts arose from engineering practice;
- contemporary mathematical reception;
- later Laplace-transform, operator-theoretic, or generalized-function interpretations must be separated from demonstrated historical influence.

Graph role: a later external-world → mathematical-method → internal-question episode, structurally comparable to Fourier without claiming the same genealogy.

This is a high-value future unit once the differential-equation / mathematical-physics lineage has enough prior graph support.

## Later intersection candidates

### Probability / measure

Potential external roots include games of chance, error theory, statistical physics, and statistics, but each must be researched separately rather than merged into one motivation story.

Graph role: eventually intersect probability with measure theory and analysis.

### Communication / information

Possible actors/contexts include Nyquist, Hartley, Shannon, and communications engineering.

Potential mathematical Questions concern representation, sampling, uncertainty, coding, and information. These should be introduced only when the mathematical Question and historical transition are sufficiently bounded.

### Functional analysis / Hilbert-space era

Graph role: later reintersection of analysis, integral equations, spectral questions, geometry-like structure, and mathematical physics.

Do not use "Hilbert space" as a retrospective label for earlier work without dating the abstraction.

### Algebraic topology / cohomology

Graph role: major later reintersection of Algebra and Topology, leading toward Serre and broader twentieth-century structural mathematics.

### Algebraic geometry / Grothendieck-era synthesis

Graph role: later reintersection of Algebra, Geometry, Number Theory, sheaf/cohomological methods, and categorical structure.

This should be reached through supported intermediate branches, not by jumping directly from nineteenth-century algebra to Grothendieck.

    ├─ motion / calculus / differential equations

    └─ geometry

External-context tributaries
    ├─ motion
    ├─ strings / continuous media
    ├─ heat
    ├─ fields / potential theory
    └─ telegraphy / operational methods

The purpose of this file is to preserve the forest-level choices. A candidate becomes an approved Research Unit only after its scope is bounded in a dedicated brief and its place in the current graph is rechecked against then-current `main`.
