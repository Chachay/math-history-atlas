# Research Unit

ID: R007
Title: Gauss — curvature, surfaces, and the emergence of intrinsic geometry
Period: approximately 1770–1850

## Purpose

Open a genuine geometry branch after R005–R006 without forcing a connection to analysis or algebra. The mathematical spine is the question of what geometric properties of a surface can be determined intrinsically, not a biography of Gauss and not a history of surveying.

## Central Atlas Question

**Can curvature be determined from measurements made within a surface itself?**

## Historical scope

Investigate:

1. What problems about surfaces, geodesy, and differential geometry Gauss was actually addressing.
2. How curvature was formulated before and in Gauss's work.
3. What the Theorema Egregium actually established and how its historical wording differs from later textbook formulations.
4. To what extent the intrinsic/extrinsic distinction is contemporary to Gauss versus a later interpretation.
5. Which earlier or parallel work is needed to avoid a single-inventor story.
6. How Gauss's surface theory can responsibly connect to later Riemannian geometry without implying a direct linear genealogy that the evidence does not support.

## Origin pattern

Mixed:

- mathematical development inside geometry;
- external context may include geodesy/surveying where historically relevant;
- the Atlas Question remains mathematical.

Apply `research/EXTERNAL-CONTEXT-GUIDELINE.md`: geodesy is context, not the Question spine.

## Historical risks to test

- "Gauss invented differential geometry."
- "Theorema Egregium introduced the modern intrinsic viewpoint" without qualification.
- Treating modern Gaussian curvature notation/definitions as Gauss's exact conceptual framework.
- Using surveying/geodesy as a causal explanation without documentary support.
- Jumping directly from Gauss to Riemann while omitting intermediaries.
